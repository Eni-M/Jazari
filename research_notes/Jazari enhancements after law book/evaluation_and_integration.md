## (a) Evaluation: phrase-matching weaknesses, cheaper and better offline checks, statistics for 45 items, public C#/.NET benchmarks, contamination

### Takeaway
A 45-question set **cannot statistically detect a 2-3 question change**. A +3 win with zero losses gives exact McNemar p = 0.25, and you need at least 6 wins and 0 losses to reach p < 0.05. Treat the test set as a coarse regression guard. Make decisions on the paired per-item win/loss table, grow the set toward 150-300 items, and add execution-based checks (compile, and for code answers, tests) wherever an answer contains code.

### Cited Findings
**Statistics for small evals**
- Anthropic's "Adding Error Bars to Evals" (Evan Miller, Nov 2024; older than 12 months) recommends comparing two models with **paired per-question differences** rather than two separate means. The correlation between the models' per-question scores determines how much this shrinks the standard error. It also recommends reporting the mean difference, SE, confidence interval and correlation for every comparison — [Anthropic research post](https://anthropic.com/research/statistical-approach-to-model-evals); [arXiv 2411.00640](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.00640)
- The same paper recommends **clustered standard errors** when questions come in groups, such as several questions sharing one passage. On popular evals the clustered SEs were sometimes more than 3x the naive values — [Anthropic post](https://anthropic.com/research/statistical-approach-to-model-evals)
- The open-source `errorbars` Python package implements paired comparisons and cluster-robust SEs (a CR1 sandwich estimator matching statsmodels) — [PyPI errorbars](https://pypi.org/project/errorbars/) (third-party, not Anthropic)
- In McNemar's test, only discordant pairs carry information. The number of discordant items is the effective sample size — [MedCalc](https://www.medcalc.org/en/book/mcnemar-test.php); [LibreTexts](https://stats.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Applied_Statistics/Mikes_Biostatistics_Book_(Dohm)/09%3A_Categorical_Data/9.6%3A_McNemar's_test)
- Fagerland et al. (BMC Med Res Methodol 2013; older) find the exact conditional McNemar test conservative with poor power. They recommend the mid-p or asymptotic variants when small violations of the nominal level are acceptable — [BMC paper](https://bmcmedresmethodol.biomedcentral.com/track/pdf/10.1186/1471-2288-13-91)
- **Computed (exact two-sided binomial McNemar):** wins/losses 3/0 gives p = 0.25; 4/0 gives 0.125; 5/0 gives 0.0625; 5/1 gives 0.22; 6/1 gives 0.125; 4/1 gives 0.375. On 45 items the 95% interval on accuracy spans about ±5-6 questions at 70-80% accuracy (Wilson interval for 36/45 = 66%-89%).
- **Computed (power of exact McNemar at α = 0.05)** for a true net gain of 6.7 pp (3 of 45). With 8.9% wins and 2.2% losses, power is 0.10 at N=45, 0.41 at N=100, 0.64 at N=150, 0.78 at N=200 and 0.93 at N=300. With pure gains (6.7% wins, 0 losses), power is 0.08 at N=45, 0.66 at N=100 and 0.94 at N=150.

**Weak tests inflate scores (applies to phrase checks too)**
- EvalPlus extended HumanEval's tests 80x (HumanEval+). The stronger tests reduced pass@k by up to 19.3-28.9% across 26 LLMs and **changed model rankings** — [ICLR/LiveCodeBench citing EvalPlus](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.07974v2) (EvalPlus paper itself not fetched; figure via secondary listing)

**Public benchmarks usable offline**
- MultiPL-E translates HumanEval and MBPP into 18 languages, including C#. HumanEval-C# has 158 problems and runs via an offline harness (dotnet build plus tests) — [MultiPL-E paper page](https://ccs.neu.edu/~arjunguha/main/papers/2023-multipl-e.html); [evalscope MultiPL-E HumanEval#](https://evalscope.readthedocs.io/en/latest/benchmarks/multiple_humaneval.html)
- Safurai-Csharp (2023; older) reports 56.3% pass@1 on a manual MultiPL-E C# run, versus GPT-3.5-Turbo at 58.2% and CodeLlama at 30.4%. The evaluation was manual, so it is not directly comparable to harness numbers — [arXiv 2311.03243](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.03243)
- LiveCodeBench dates every problem and evaluates only on windows after a model's training cutoff. Scores drop sharply after cutoff, mostly on LeetCode problems, which shows contamination. It correlates about 0.72 with HumanEval+, and some fine-tuned models do well on HumanEval+ but poorly on LiveCodeBench (overfitting to the older benchmark) — [LiveCodeBench arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.07974v2); [ICLR 2025](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/94074dd5a072d28ff75a76dabed43767-Abstract-Conference.html). It is Python-only in its released form.

**Small LLM-as-judge reliability**
- An AMCIS 2026 paper reports position bias (the first option was preferred 65.7% of the time), self-preference of up to +73.3 pp when a model judges its own output, and only moderate inter-judge agreement (Fleiss' κ = 0.357) — [AMCIS 2026](https://aisel.aisnet.org/amcis2026/sig_svs/svs/1)
- Position bias varies a lot by judge and task — [arXiv 2406.07791](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.07791v3)
- Practitioner guidance: swap the answer order and count only wins that survive both orders, use a judge from a different model family, and check agreement against a human-labelled sample first — [Grepture blog](https://grepture.com/blog/llm-as-a-judge-bias); [koji.so](https://www.koji.so/docs/llm-as-a-judge-vs-human-evaluation) (secondary; their κ ranges of 0.6-0.8 for well-defined tasks and 0.4-0.6 for open-ended quality are unverified)

### Inferences
- **Highest-value, low-effort change:** Have run_eval print the per-item 2x2 table (wins, losses, ties) against the baseline and the exact McNemar p-value. Change the decision rule from "score went up" to "wins > losses, no must_not regressions, p reported". Effort: about 1 hour. Evidence: strong (standard statistics).
- **Grow the test set** to 150+ items, mainly from real misses in the usage log (see b), keeping the dev/test split. Until then, describe a +2/+3 change as "consistent with noise". Use temperature 0 or a fixed seed. If sampling is used, run k ≥ 3 samples and average per item, as the Anthropic paper advises for reducing variance.
- **Execution-based checks for code answers:** extract the fenced C# blocks, wrap them in a template project, and run `dotnet build`. Score compile success and, for some items, small xUnit asserts. On a T480 a warm `dotnet build` of a tiny project takes seconds. Keeping one reusable project and replacing a single file avoids a fresh restore each time. Effort: 1-2 days. Evidence: strong (EvalPlus shows weak checks mis-rank models).
- **Phrase-matching weaknesses:** it rewards keyword stuffing, misses paraphrases, ignores correctness of code and order of reasoning, and can be gamed by prompt tuning on the dev set. Keep it, but add regex/alternatives per must_have, a compile check, and a short "key claim" checklist. A local judge is a poor fit for Jazari's 10 tok/s budget and has documented biases. If used at all, restrict it to pairwise A/B with order swapping on the dev set, after a 30-item human-agreement check.
- **MultiPL-E HumanEval-C#** (158 problems) is the only well-known offline C#-executable benchmark found. It tests code generation, not ASP.NET or security Q&A, so use it as a secondary "did general C# coding regress" signal (about 158 × 20-60 s ≈ 1-2.5 h per run on CPU). Its problems are public and likely in pretraining data, so absolute numbers are inflated. Deltas between Jazari configurations are still meaningful.
- **Contamination hygiene for the private set:** never paste test items into prompts, the law book, few-shot examples or fine-tuning data. Keep a hash list of test items and grep any new training or RAG corpus against it. Rotate in fresh items dated after each tuning round, in the spirit of LiveCodeBench's time windows.

### Gaps
- No .NET-specific Q&A benchmark (ASP.NET Core, security) with public, offline-runnable scoring was found. CRUXEval has no C# variant in the results.
- Primary EvalPlus, Chatbot Arena (Bradley-Terry) and HELM pages were not fetched because of the network block. No peer-reviewed study of ≤7B judges specifically was found.

## (b) Using its own usage logs (accept/reject flags, logged Q&A) safely

### Takeaway
For a single user, the log is most valuable as a **source of new test and dev items from real misses**, and as a running acceptance-rate metric per question category. Preference training comes later. Binary accept/reject labels fit KTO directly, while DPO needs paired chosen/rejected answers. Hundreds of labelled items are needed before training is worth trying.

### Cited Findings
- KTO trains on single responses labelled desirable or undesirable and does not need pairs. DPO needs (chosen, rejected) pairs for the same prompt. The KTO paper (ICML 2024; older) reports matching or beating DPO from 1B to 30B parameters using only binary signals, and robustness to heavy imbalance (up to 90% of desirable examples dropped) — [KTO arXiv 2402.01306](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.01306v1.pdf); [PremAI summary](https://blog.premai.io/which-llm-alignment-method-rlhf-vs-dpo-vs-kto-tradeoffs-explained) (no independent replication found)
- Continue logs "dev data" locally to `~/.continue/dev_data/{version}/{eventName}.jsonl`. A `data` block sets the destination (`file://` or HTTP), and a `level` of `all` or `noCode` decides whether prefixes, suffixes and completions are stored. Autocomplete events record acceptance/rejection, timing and cache hits — [Continue dev-data guide (Gitea mirror of repo)](https://gitea.varghacsongor.hu/GithubMirror/continue/src/branch/docs/customize-tab-restructure/.continue/rules/dev-data-guide.md)
- GitHub Copilot usage metrics come exclusively from IDE telemetry (users with telemetry off are not counted). Acceptance rate is the headline trust and relevance signal — [GitHub Docs: Copilot usage metrics](https://docs.github.com/en/copilot/concepts/copilot-usage-metrics/copilot-metrics). A GitHub-linked 2023 study found 28.9% of suggestions accepted in the first 3 months, rising to 32.1% (older) — [ITPro](https://www.itpro.com/technology/artificial-intelligence/github-30-of-copilot-coding-suggestions-are-accepted)
- Third-party guides warn that acceptance measures adoption more than quality, and that accept-then-delete may inflate it — [Jellyfish](https://jellyfish.co/library/github-copilot-monitoring/) (secondary)
- Tabby markets usage reports, analytics and an "enforce IDE telemetry policy" feature. The exact events it collects were not confirmed, and secondary sources conflict ("no telemetry" vs "can be disabled") — [Railway Tabby](https://railway.com/deploy/tabby); [everydev](https://www.everydev.ai/tools/tabby)

### Inferences
- **Miss-to-item pipeline (high value, about 1 day):** a weekly script pulls rejected answers from SQLite and clusters them by topic tag or keywords (ASP.NET, networking, security, /edit, /review). The user turns about 5 good misses a week into new items with must_have/must_not, assigned to the **dev** set by default. A fixed fraction (for example every 4th item) goes to the test set, chosen before anyone looks at the model's tuned answer, to avoid selection bias.
- **Add implicit signals** to the log: whether the user re-asked within 2 minutes, whether an /edit diff was applied and kept, and answer length and latency. These are better than a single flag, mirroring the acceptance and retention signals products track. Add the gateway build, prompt or law-book version and model hash per row, so acceptance rates can be compared across releases (with the same small-N caution as in (a)).
- **Preference data later:** store the rejected answer plus the user's correction or accepted re-answer, which gives DPO/ORPO-style pairs. Rejects alone are KTO-ready. Training on CPU is not practical. This would be a later free-GPU job (see the existing "free_gpu_finetuning" notes). Never include test-set prompts.
- **Privacy:** the log can contain source code, secrets and customer data. Keep it local, scrub obvious secrets (API keys, connection strings) on write with regexes, add a retention limit and a `/forget` command, and keep it out of the git repo. Offer a `noCode`-style level as Continue does.
- **Bias warning:** one user's rejects reflect their question mix. A rising acceptance rate after a prompt change can come from a shift in topics. Compare only within the same category.

### Gaps
- Copilot's use of thumbs-up/down feedback for model improvement was not found in official docs. Tabby's event schema was not confirmed. No study was found on preference tuning from a single user's small log.

## (c) Integrations worth building, and what each needs from the gateway

### Takeaway
Because the gateway already speaks OpenAI `/v1/chat/completions`, most IDE chat integrations need only **SSE streaming in the exact OpenAI chunk format**, a `/v1/models` listing, and (for agent modes) OpenAI-style `tools`/`tool_calls`. Inline completion needs a separate FIM path (`/v1/completions` with `suffix`, or llama.cpp `/infill`) and a FIM-trained model. At about 10 tok/s on a 7B-class hybrid model, inline completion is marginal. Chat, edit and review integrations are the better value.

### Cited Findings
- **Continue:** models are configured in `~/.continue/config.yaml` with `provider: openai` plus `apiBase`, and roles per model (chat, edit, autocomplete). For autocomplete, a `provider: llama.cpp` entry pointing at a llama-server works, with `autocompleteOptions` (debounceDelay, maxPromptTokens, modelTimeout). Set `apiBase` explicitly, or the key goes to OpenAI — [DEV: FIM with llama.cpp in Continue](https://dev.to/vast-cow/getting-fill-in-the-middle-autocomplete-working-in-vs-code-continue-with-llamacpp-552h); [UCSB Continue guide](https://docs.cloud.college.ucsb.edu/AI-LLM/API-Access/Coding-Agents/Continue-VSCode) (secondary; official docs not fetched). With LM Studio, Continue's tab autocomplete calls `/v1/completions` with a `suffix` parameter and works with FIM-trained coder models (Qwen2.5-Coder, DeepSeek-Coder) — [DEV LM Studio guide](https://dev.to/jovan_chan_9500711396d4e6/continuedev-lm-studio-2026-setup-guide-the-context-window-dial-you-must-set-before-loading-29lb)
- **VS Code (Copilot Chat BYOK):** BYOK puts external and local models (Ollama auto-discovery) in the Chat model picker. Per a Requesty guide, the **Custom Endpoint provider became stable and BYOK stopped requiring GitHub sign-in in VS Code 1.122 (May 28 2026)**. When adding an endpoint you choose its API: Chat Completions, Responses or Messages. Config lives in `chatLanguageModels.json`/settings. A model that does not declare tool support disappears from Agent mode — [Requesty BYOK guide](https://docs.requesty.ai/integrations/github-copilot.md); [kunalganglani](https://www.kunalganglani.com/blog/run-local-llm-vscode) (secondary; verify against VS Code docs)
- **Visual Studio:** the Sept 29 2026 update brought a "Bring Your Own Model" preview working in Agent Mode with Foundry, OpenAI, Anthropic and Ollama, including **custom endpoints for OpenAI and Ollama**. Model capabilities vary, so not every model supports every Agent capability — [Microsoft DevBlogs Sept 2026](https://devblogs.microsoft.com/visualstudio/visual-studio-september-update-power-your-workflow-with-your-model). In the hands-on review, the Ollama endpoint defaulted to `http://127.0.0.1:11434/v1` and Visual Studio flagged whether a model "supports Tools", which Agent mode needs — [Visual Studio Magazine, Aug 2026](https://visualstudiomagazine.com/articles/2026/08/25/hands-on-previewing-bring-your-own-local-ai-model-to-visual-studio-insiders.aspx)
- **JetBrains (Rider) AI Assistant 2026.2:** Settings | Tools | AI Assistant | Providers & API keys lets you add LM Studio, Ollama or an OpenAI-compatible URL, then assign local models to features. Without that assignment they appear only in chat. **Inline completion can use a separate OpenAI-compatible endpoint, which Test Connection does not verify.** An "offline mode" help page exists — [JetBrains help: custom models](https://www.jetbrains.com/help/ai-assistant/use-custom-models.html); [offline mode](https://www.jetbrains.com/de-de/help/ai-assistant/switching-to-offline-mode.html)
- **Cline / Roo Code:** Roo uses native OpenAI tool calling only when the endpoint mirrors OpenAI exactly, otherwise it falls back to XML-in-text tools (forceable in settings). Cline treats tool calling as required and cannot auto-detect a custom endpoint's capabilities. Small local models struggle with tool formatting and long context — [Roo Code docs: OpenAI compatible](https://docs.roocode.com/providers/openai-compatible); [haimaker Cline guide](https://haimaker.ai/blog/cline-custom-provider-setup/) (secondary)
- **FIM on llama.cpp:** `/infill` takes `input_prefix`/`input_suffix` and applies the model's FIM tokens on the server, so it needs a FIM-capable model. llama-server supports speculative decoding with a small draft model (PR #10455). One CPU report found server speculative decoding 10-15% slower than `llama-speculative-simple` — [llama-server endpoints tour](https://mvysny.github.io/llama-server-endpoints/); [PR 10455](https://github.com/ggerganov/llama.cpp/pull/10455); [issue 12968](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/issues/12968). No CPU latency benchmark for llama.vscode FIM was found.
- **MCP:** the 2025-11-25 spec defines stdio (the client launches the server as a subprocess, JSON-RPC over stdin/stdout; clients SHOULD support stdio) and Streamable HTTP (which replaced the 2024-11-05 HTTP+SSE transport) — [MCP spec: transports](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2025-11-25/basic/transports)

### Inferences (ranked by value/effort for one developer)
1. **Continue (VS Code and JetBrains) or VS Code BYOK Custom Endpoint for chat and edit.** Value: high. Effort: low, if streaming SSE (`data: {chunk}\n\n` with `choices[0].delta`, final `data: [DONE]`), `/v1/models`, and Bearer auth already match OpenAI. Test with the official OpenAI .NET/JS SDK as a conformance client. Evidence: medium (docs partly secondary).
2. **Visual Studio BYOM via "custom OpenAI endpoint".** Value: high for an ASP.NET developer. Effort: low to medium. It is preview and Agent-mode-centric, so it likely needs tool-calling support, and Granite 4.0-H Tiny's tool reliability at 10 tok/s should be tested. Evidence: medium (Microsoft blog plus one hands-on review).
3. **Jazari as an MCP server (stdio, written in C# with the official C# MCP SDK)** exposing tools such as `jazari_ask`, `jazari_review_file` and `law_book_search`. Any MCP client (VS Code, Visual Studio, Claude Desktop, Cline) can then call Jazari's curated knowledge without Jazari having to be the chat model. Value: medium-high. Effort: 1-2 days. Stdio avoids network exposure entirely. Evidence: strong for the spec, unknown for client behaviour with a slow tool (needs generous timeouts).
4. **Tool calling (`tools`/`tool_calls`) in the gateway.** This is needed for Cline, Copilot Agent mode and Visual Studio Agent. llama.cpp's `--jinja` chat-template tool support can be passed through. Value: medium. Effort: medium. Risk: small models often break tool formats (Roo/Cline docs). The XML fallback in Roo is the more forgiving client.
5. **FIM inline completion.** Value: low to medium on this hardware. Completion needs roughly a few hundred ms for 20-50 tokens to feel usable, and 10 tok/s gives 2-5 s. This would need a separate tiny FIM-trained coder model (0.5-1.5B) on llama-server `/infill` or `/v1/completions` with `suffix`, with Continue, llama.vscode or JetBrains' separate completion endpoint as the client. Granite 4.0-H Tiny's FIM support is unverified. Evidence: weak (no CPU benchmark found; latency target is an inference).

### Gaps
- Official Continue, VS Code language-model API and Visual Studio docs could not be fetched, so exact config keys and versions are from secondary sources. No source confirmed whether VS Code BYOK supports completions (inline) as well as chat.
- No measured FIM latency on CPU-only laptops was found.

## (d) Security of a local assistant gateway (localhost exposure, prompt injection via files, OWASP LLM Top 10)

### Takeaway
The realistic threats for a single-user local tool are (1) **web pages reaching the localhost API** through DNS rebinding or cross-origin requests, (2) **indirect prompt injection from files and repos Jazari reads**, which becomes dangerous once /edit or tools can write files or run commands, and (3) secrets leaking into logs. Bind to 127.0.0.1, require the API key on every route, validate Origin/Host, and keep a human approval step on every write or command.

### Cited Findings
- The MCP spec (2025-11-25) says servers MUST validate the `Origin` header on all connections to prevent DNS rebinding, returning 403 if it is invalid. Local servers SHOULD bind only to 127.0.0.1, not all interfaces, and SHOULD require authentication. Without this, a remote website can reach a local server — [MCP spec: transports](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2025-11-25/basic/transports)
- OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications 2025: LLM01 Prompt Injection (direct and indirect), LLM02 Sensitive Information Disclosure, LLM03 Supply Chain, LLM04 Data and Model Poisoning, LLM05 Improper Output Handling, LLM06 Excessive Agency (expanded for agentic tools), LLM07 System Prompt Leakage, LLM08 Vector and Embedding Weaknesses, LLM09 Misinformation, LLM10 Unbounded Consumption — [Invicti summary](https://www.invicti.com/blog/web-security/owasp-top-10-risks-llm-security-2025); [Open Source Security deep dive](https://opensourcesecurity.substack.com/p/a-deep-dive-into-the-owasp-top-10) (secondary; official genai.owasp.org not fetched)
- HiddenLayer (Jul 2025) showed that a hidden comment in a cloned repo's README made Cursor's agent search for API keys and exfiltrate them, bypassing a curl denylist. The injection was invisible on GitHub — [HiddenLayer](https://www.hiddenlayer.com/research/how-hidden-prompt-injections-can-hijack-ai-code-assistants-like-cursor)
- CVE-2025-53773 (GitHub Copilot agent mode, CVSS 7.8): instructions hidden in a README, source file or issue led to local file writes and RCE. The "IDEsaster" research reported 30+ vulnerabilities and 24 CVEs across 10+ AI IDE products — [ARMO](https://www.armosec.io/blog/indirect-prompt-injection-examples/); [BeyondMachines](https://beyondmachines.net/event_details/researchers-report-a-vulnerability-class-in-ai-powered-development-tools-dubbed-idesaster-r-m-u-i-9) (secondary CVE mappings); a CSA research note covers README instruction injection across Copilot, Cursor, Claude Code, Windsurf and Roo Code — [CSA note, Mar 2026](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/CSA_research_note_readme_instruction_injection_ai_coding_agents_20260317-csa-styled.pdf)

### Inferences (OWASP items that apply to Jazari)
- **LLM01 (indirect injection via /read, /review, law book):** wrap file contents in clear delimiters labelled "untrusted data", strip or flag invisible Unicode and HTML comments, and never let file text trigger tools automatically. Value: high. Effort: low.
- **LLM05/LLM06 (output handling, excessive agency):** /edit must show a diff and require explicit confirmation. Allow no shell execution without approval. Restrict writes to the opened project root and block path traversal. Never render model output as HTML in any web UI.
- **LLM02 (sensitive data):** scrub secrets before writing to the SQLite log, keep the DB outside synced folders, and file-permission it to the user.
- **LLM10 (unbounded consumption):** the existing queue should cap max_tokens, queue length and per-request timeout, so a runaway client (for example an agent loop in Cline) cannot pin the CPU.
- **LLM03 (supply chain):** pin the GGUF by SHA-256 and pin the llama.cpp build and NuGet packages.
- **Gateway hardening checklist (about half a day):** Kestrel `ListenLocalhost` only; API key required even on localhost, so browsers and other local apps cannot use it freely; reject requests whose `Origin` is not absent or allow-listed and whose `Host` is not `127.0.0.1`/`localhost` (anti-DNS-rebinding, following the MCP spec's rule); no permissive CORS; no secrets in logs.
- LLM07 (system prompt leakage) and LLM08 (embeddings) are low risk for a single user, apart from avoiding secrets in the system prompt and law book.

### Gaps
- The official OWASP 2025 document text was not fetched, so the mitigations above are mapped by inference. No CVE data specific to llama.cpp server exposure was gathered in this pass.
